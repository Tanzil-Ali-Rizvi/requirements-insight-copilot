# Delivery promise and customer communication policy

*Illustrative policy written for this case study. It is not Olist's actual policy.*

## Promised delivery date

The promised date is shown at checkout and in the order confirmation. A delivery is on time when the parcel is delivered on or before the promised date; delivered after it, the order is late.

## How the promise is set

The promise comes from the delivery-estimate service (FR-01): the 90th-percentile delivery time of the order's route, scaled up when recent deliveries are slower than normal. The model is retrained monthly. Changes to the method need approval from the Head of Operations.

## Running-late notice

When an order's transit time passes the route's 80th-percentile transit time, the customer gets one in-app and email notice with a revised date. Customer experience owns the wording.

## Compensation

Orders delivered more than 5 days after the promised date qualify for a freight refund on request. Customer support handles requests within 2 business days.

## Peak seasons

November (Black Friday) and February to March are peak seasons. Logistics books extra carrier capacity for them, starting with North and Northeast lanes, using the forecast delivered each September and January.

## Reporting

Late rate, handoff rate and transit days are reported weekly from the governed metric layer. The on-time target is a late rate of 5.0% or lower.
